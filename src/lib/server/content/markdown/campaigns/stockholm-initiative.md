The **Stockholm Initiative for Nuclear Disarmament** is a government-level diplomatic initiative launched in 2019 by 16 non-nuclear weapon states from different regions of the world, including Sweden, Germany, Japan, Jordan, Argentina, Canada, Indonesia, and New Zealand.

The initiative was created to break the prolonged deadlock in multilateral disarmament diplomacy and prepare pragmatic, near-term measures to strengthen the Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT).

## The Stepping Stones Approach

Rather than waiting for broad political consensus, the Stockholm Initiative formulated **24 Stepping Stones**—practical, verifiable actions that nuclear-armed states can undertake immediately:
- Lowering the operational readiness of nuclear weapons systems (de-alerting).
- Reducing the role of nuclear weapons in military doctrines and national security strategies.
- Increasing transparency regarding warhead numbers, fissile material stockpiles, and modernization budgets.
- Halting the production of weapons-grade fissile material and expanding disarmament verification research.

## Role in Global Disarmament

The Stockholm Initiative acts as an essential diplomatic bridge between traditional NPT review mechanisms and more progressive disarmament treaties, demonstrating that non-nuclear armed states have both the right and the responsibility to shape international security architecture.
