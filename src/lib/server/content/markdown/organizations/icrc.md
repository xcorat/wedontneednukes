The **International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC)** is an impartial, neutral, and independent humanitarian organization established under the Geneva Conventions to protect the lives and dignity of victims of armed conflict.

The ICRC and the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement have played a decisive historical role in nuclear disarmament:
- In August 1945, ICRC delegate Marcel Junod was the first foreign medical professional to reach Hiroshima, documenting the devastation and delivering fifteen tons of essential medical supplies to dying victims.
- Since that time, the ICRC has maintained that any use of nuclear weapons is fundamentally incompatible with the principles and rules of **International Humanitarian Law (IHL)**, particularly the principles of distinction, proportionality, and the prohibition of weapons causing unnecessary suffering or severe environmental damage.

## Four Key Findings of the Red Cross Movement

1. Nuclear weapons are catastrophic in their effects and cannot distinguish between combatants and civilians.
2. The blast, heat, and radiation effects cause agonizing suffering lasting across generations through genetic abnormalities and radiation-induced cancers.
3. There is currently no adequate international humanitarian response capacity to assist the victims of a nuclear detonation.
4. Total elimination is the only reliable guarantee that nuclear weapons will never be detonated again.
