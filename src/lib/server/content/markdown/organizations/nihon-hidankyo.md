**Nihon Hidankyo (The Japan Confederation of A- and H-Bomb Sufferers Organizations)** is the sole nationwide confederation of atomic bomb survivors (*hibakusha*) from Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Founded in August 1956 in the wake of the 1954 Bikini Atoll thermonuclear test, Hidankyo represents tens of thousands of survivors and their families.

In October 2024, Nihon Hidankyo was awarded the **2024 Nobel Peace Prize** *"for its efforts to achieve a world free of nuclear weapons and for demonstrating through witness testimony that nuclear weapons must never be used again."*

## The Moral Taboo Against Nuclear Weapons

For nearly seven decades, Hidankyo has provided thousands of witness accounts, published survivor memoirs, mounted traveling photo exhibitions, and dispatched annual delegations to the United Nations and international diplomatic summits.

Through the relentless sharing of firsthand physical and psychological agony, the hibakusha established an international moral norm—the **nuclear taboo**—that stigmatizes the use and possession of nuclear weapons as a crime against all humanity.

## Two Fundamental Demands

1. **State Compensation & Care**: Demanding that the Japanese government acknowledge state responsibility for initiating the war and provide comprehensive medical and livelihood compensation to all radiation victims.
2. **Never Again for Any People**: Preventing any future generation on Earth from ever enduring the hellish suffering of nuclear fire and radiation sickness.
