## AI & Brittle Systems

Decision-support software already shapes nuclear command pipelines.
An automated recommendation does not absolve human operators of
responsibility — but it shortens the window for second-guessing.

## Crisis of Leadership

Several nuclear-armed states are governed by leaders with aggressive
rhetoric, internal political instability, or both. Stable deterrence
depends on stable decision-makers.

## Breaking the Proliferation Trap

Treaty regimes are weakening. New entrants are easier than ever. The
non-proliferation bargain — "you keep yours, we don't build ours" —
is fraying.

## Multiple Flashpoints

Active conflicts sit close to nuclear-armed states. Borders drawn in
the 20th century are disputed in the 21st, and several disputes
involve at least one declared nuclear state.

## Lowered Tech Barriers

Hyper-sonic delivery, autonomous subsystems, and dual-use scientific
leaks make the technical threshold for credible deterrent posture
lower than it once was — which paradoxically raises instability.
