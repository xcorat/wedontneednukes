The **ICAN Cities Appeal** is a global municipal advocacy initiative launched by the International Campaign to Abolish Nuclear Weapons (ICAN). It provides a mechanism for local councils, metropolitan assemblies, and mayors to officially declare their support for the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (TPNW) and pressure their national federal governments to sign and ratify the treaty.

## Why Cities Take the Lead

Because nuclear weapons are designed to incinerate urban populations and destroy civilian infrastructure, municipal governments are on the frontlines of humanitarian risk:
- Cities have no adequate healthcare, disaster relief, or civil defense capacity to respond to even a single nuclear detonation.
- Municipal endorsements build democratic consensus from the bottom up, challenging central government deterrence orthodoxy.

## Major Participating Cities

Hundreds of prominent world cities have formally passed council resolutions endorsing the Cities Appeal, including:
- **Paris, Berlin, Rome, Amsterdam, Oslo, Bern, and Helsinki** in Europe.
- **Washington D.C., Los Angeles, New York City, and Toronto** in North America.
- **Sydney, Melbourne, and Canberra** in Australia.
- **Hiroshima and Nagasaki** in Japan.

## How Local Citizens Can Act

Local residents can introduce the standardized ICAN Cities Appeal resolution to their municipal council or city clerk, present testimonies at town hall sessions, and coordinate with local peace and social justice organizations to secure passage.
