**Gensuikin (Japan Congress Against A- and H-Bombs)** is one of Japan’s leading grassroots citizen movements campaigning for the complete elimination of nuclear arms and the phase-out of commercial nuclear power.

The movement traces its roots to the national outrage sparked by the March 1, 1954 United States thermonuclear detonation (*Castle Bravo*) at Bikini Atoll, which irradiated the crew of the Japanese tuna fishing vessel *Daigo Fukuryū Maru* (Lucky Dragon No. 5) and contaminated fish supplies across Japan.

## Fundamental Principles & Actions

- **Annual World Conference**: Convening annual world conferences in Hiroshima and Nagasaki every August, bringing together international peace delegates, labor unions, and radiation survivors from around the globe.
- **Global Hibakusha Support**: Extending humanitarian support and legal recognition not only to atomic bomb survivors of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, but to nuclear test victims and uranium mining communities across the South Pacific, the Marshall Islands, Kazakhstan, and the southwestern United States.
- **Nuclear-Free Future**: Opposing both military nuclear arsenals and the catastrophic environmental risks of civilian nuclear power, particularly highlighted after the 2011 Fukushima Daiichi disaster.
