The **International Campaign to Abolish Nuclear Weapons (ICAN)** is a broad-based, global civil society coalition dedicated to achieving the total prohibition and elimination of nuclear weapons. Originally launched in Melbourne, Australia by the International Physicians for the Prevention of Nuclear War (IPPNW) in 2007, ICAN today connects more than 650 non-governmental partner organizations spanning over 110 countries.

ICAN was awarded the **2017 Nobel Peace Prize** *"for its work to draw attention to the catastrophic humanitarian consequences of any use of nuclear weapons and for its ground-breaking efforts to achieve a treaty-based prohibition of such weapons."*

## The Humanitarian Initiative & The TPNW

ICAN played the central coordinating role in shifting the global disarmament debate away from traditional state-centric deterrence narratives toward the catastrophic, indiscriminate humanitarian and environmental consequences of nuclear detonations. This coalition-driven diplomatic momentum culminated on July 7, 2017, when 122 states voted to adopt the **Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (TPNW)** at the United Nations in New York. The treaty formally entered into international legal force on January 22, 2021.

## Core Campaign Programs

- **TPNW Universalization**: Working continuously with non-nuclear states to sign and ratify the ban treaty, stigmatizing the possession and testing of nuclear arms.
- **[ICAN Cities Appeal](https://cities.icanw.org)**: Mobilizing hundreds of municipal governments—including Paris, Berlin, Washington D.C., and Sydney—to pass council resolutions endorsing the TPNW.
- **[ICAN Parliamentary Pledge](https://pledge.icanw.org)**: Organizing thousands of sitting national legislators across nuclear-armed, nuclear-umbrella, and non-aligned states to commit to disarmament legislation.
- **[Don’t Bank on the Bomb](https://www.dontbankonthebomb.com)**: Tracking financial institutions investing in nuclear weapons producers and coordinating divestment initiatives.

## Strategic Vision

ICAN maintains that nuclear weapons cannot protect against modern existential crises—such as pandemics, climate disruption, or cyberwarfare—and instead present an intolerable threat of accidental, unauthorized, or intentional global destruction.
