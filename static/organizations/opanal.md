**OPANAL (Organismo para la Proscripción de las Armas Nucleares en la América Latina y el Caribe)** is the specialized intergovernmental organization established by the pioneering **Treaty of Tlatelolco** in 1967.

The Treaty of Tlatelolco made history by establishing the world's first **Nuclear-Weapon-Free Zone (NWFZ)** in a densely populated region, encompassing all 33 sovereign states of Latin America and the Caribbean.

## The Tlatelolco Precedent

Following the harrowing 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis, Mexican diplomat Alfonso García Robles (awarded the 1982 Nobel Peace Prize) led the diplomatic campaign that persuaded regional states to renounce the acquisition, testing, or storage of nuclear weapons.

## OPANAL's Continuing Role

- **Verification & Safeguards**: Ensuring that all 33 member states adhere strictly to their non-nuclear status, in close coordination with the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA).
- **Legally Binding Protocols**: Securing and monitoring compliance with Additional Protocols signed by declared nuclear weapon states (US, Russia, China, UK, France), legally binding them never to use or threaten to use nuclear weapons against Latin American and Caribbean territory.
- **Inspirational Model**: Serving as the structural blueprint for subsequent NWFZs created in the South Pacific (Rarotonga), Southeast Asia (Bangkok), Africa (Pelindaba), and Central Asia (Semipalatinsk).
