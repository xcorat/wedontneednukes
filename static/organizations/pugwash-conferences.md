The **Pugwash Conferences on Science and World Affairs** is an international non-governmental organization founded in 1957 in the village of Pugwash, Nova Scotia, Canada. The organization was born from the **Russell-Einstein Manifesto** of 1955, signed by Albert Einstein, Bertrand Russell, Max Born, Linus Pauling, and Joseph Rotblat, which called upon world scientists to *"remember your humanity, and forget the rest"* to prevent thermonuclear oblivion.

Pugwash and its long-time president, Sir Joseph Rotblat, were jointly awarded the **1995 Nobel Peace Prize** *"for their efforts to diminish the part played by nuclear arms in international politics and, in the longer run, to eliminate such arms."*

## Track-II Scientific Diplomacy

Throughout the Cold War and in contemporary crises, Pugwash has served as an indispensable channel of informal, track-II diplomatic dialogue between scientists and high-level officials from opposing power blocs:
- Technical consultations developed the scientific foundations for the **Partial Test Ban Treaty (PTBT)** in 1963 and the **Anti-Ballistic Missile (ABM) Treaty** in 1972.
- Today, Pugwash focuses on verifiable dismantlement methodologies, regional dialogues in South Asia and the Middle East, and the dangers of integrating automated AI decision-making into nuclear command architectures.
